Off-Season Basketball Program — Strength, Power & Leg Athleticism

Profile: Beginner lifter, 190cm / 100kg, full gym access, off-season, 3 lifts + 2 basketball sessions/week
Focus: Balanced athletic performance, injury durability, and fixing leg mobility/explosiveness weaknesses

Weekly Layout

Day Session

Tue Basketball Skills/Conditioning
Wed Lift B — Upper Body + Core
Thu Basketball Skills/Conditioning
Fri Lift C — Lower Body Strength + Plyo
Sat Active recovery / optional light mobility
Sun Full rest

Swap days freely — the key rule is never stack Lift A/C back-to-back without a rest or upper day between, since both hit the legs hard.

Daily Mobility Primer (do before EVERY session — lift or ball, ~8-10 min)

Since leg mobility/athleticism is a weak point, this block is non-negotiable, not optional warm-up filler:

  1. Ankle dorsiflexion rocks (knee-to-wall) — 2x10 each side
  2. 90/90 hip switches — 2x8 each side
  3. Couch stretch (hip flexor) — 45 sec each side
  4. World's greatest stretch — 5 each side
  5. Lateral lunge to hamstring sweep — 8 each side
  6. Band monster walks (lateral + forward) — 2x10 steps

This alone will move the needle on "bottom tier" leg movement quality within a few weeks — it's addressing hip/ankle range of motion, which is usually the real bottleneck, not just strength.

Starting Weights

Barbell weights are the total including the 20 kg bar. These are conservative estimates for a beginner of your size coming back after 2 months off. I don't know your actual strength, so treat them as a starting point.

  • Week 1: do 2 sets per exercise at the low end of each range, so the soreness stays manageable and the technique comes back first
  • Week 2: go back to the full sets. If the last rep of each set feels like you had 3 or more reps left, move up to the middle of the range
  • Week 3 onward: pick the weight that leaves 2 reps in reserve on the last set, then follow the progression notes below
  • If a set is a grind with form breaking down, drop the weight 10%
  • Rest times on the jumps are about recovery, not conditioning. Wait until you feel fresh before the next set

Basketball Skills Days (Tue/Thu)

Structure however fits your skill work, but bookend with:

  • Before: the mobility primer above + 5 min of low-intensity lateral shuffles/carioca to prime hips
  • After: 10 min light plyo circuit if legs aren't fried from the session (optional, rotate weekly):
    • Single-leg hops in place — 2x10/leg
    • Broad jump — 3x3
    • Lateral skater bounds — 3x6/side

If it's a heavy scrimmage/conditioning day, skip the after-block and just stretch.

Progression Notes

  • Weeks 1-3: Establish technique on all lifts, moderate loads, submaximal jumps
  • Weeks 4-6: Increase load on main lifts (+2.5-5kg where form allows), increase box jump height slightly
  • Weeks 7-9: Introduce depth jumps if not already comfortable; this is where reactive/elastic leg power really develops
  • Deload every 4th week (cut volume ~40%) to keep joints fresh

Simple leg mobility check-ins

Every 3-4 weeks, test:

  • Ankle dorsiflexion (knee-to-wall distance)
  • Overhead squat depth/quality
  • Single-leg balance time (eyes closed)

Tracking these will show you the mobility work is actually working, since it's often not visible on a scale or in the mirror.

Equipment: 2x14kg KB, 1x10kg KB, 2x8kg DB, ab roller, heavy black band, 35kg-rated long band (no pull-up bar), bodyweight
Focus: Balanced athletic performance, injury durability, fixing leg mobility/explosiveness

A Note on Your Frame

At 191cm/95kg you're carrying real leverage and mass — that's an advantage for basketball but it also means bodyweight-only lower body work (lunges, split squats) will already feel loaded without any added weight. Don't rush to add the kettlebells to leg work until bodyweight versions feel genuinely easy with good depth and control. As a beginner, technique and range of motion come first — the strength will follow fast once the pattern is right.

Progression Notes (Beginner Pace)

  • Weeks 1-4: Master bodyweight and light-KB technique on every movement before adding load. Depth and control over speed.

Equipment: 1 kettlebell (14kg), heavy black resistance band, long 35kg-rated resistance band, bodyweight only
Focus: Balanced athletic performance, injury durability, fixing leg mobility/explosiveness

A Note on This Setup

With one kettlebell and two bands, most of your loading now comes from bodyweight + band tension rather than external weight. That's actually fine at beginner level — bodyweight and band-resisted work builds real strength and control, and your frame means bodyweight movements alone carry plenty of load to start. The kettlebell becomes your one "heavy" tool, so it gets used where it matters most: squats, hinges, and presses.

Progression Notes (Beginner Pace)

  • Weeks 1-4: Nail bodyweight technique everywhere — depth on squats, control on split squats/step-ups, full range on band rows. The kettlebell work should feel challenging by the last 2-3 reps but not grindy.
  • Weeks 5-8: Increase reps (e.g. goblet squat to 4x15) before anything else — with one kettlebell, rep progression is your main lever once bodyweight/band work plateaus.
  • Weeks 9-12: Consider tempo work (3-4 sec lowering phase) on squats and RDLs to keep adding difficulty without more load. This is also the point to reassess whether a second/heavier kettlebell would unlock more progress.
  • Deload every 4th week (lighter effort, fewer sets)

  • Bodyweight squat depth/quality (thighs parallel, heels flat)
  • Single-leg balance time, eyes closed

What's Different From the Full Home Setup

  • Dumbbell work (renegade rows, split squat loading) is now bodyweight or single-KB based
  • Ab roller swapped for push-ups and band Pallof press — still trains anti-extension/anti-rotation core, just without the tool
  • Reps are pushed higher across most lifts (8-15 vs 6-8) to compensate for only having one load option — this keeps the stimulus meaningful without needing heavier weight

Full Body Workout

Profile: Beginner, 190cm / 100kg, full gym access
Time: about 60 minutes including the warm-up

A standalone full-body session for the gym. Use it on its own or as a fill-in when you miss a lift day. Don't stack it directly on a Lift A or Lift C day, since it loads the legs too.

Warm-up (10 min)

Use the same mobility primer as the main program:

  1. Ankle dorsiflexion rocks — 2x10 each side
  2. 90/90 hip switches — 2x8 each side
  3. Couch stretch — 45 sec each side
  4. World's greatest stretch — 5 each side
  5. Lateral lunge to hamstring sweep — 8 each side
  6. Band lateral walks — 2x10 steps each direction

Then ramp up on the squat before the working sets: empty bar x10, about 50% of working weight x5, about 70% x3. One warm-up set is enough for each later exercise.

Main Session (about 50 min)

Exercise Sets x Reps Suggested start Rest Notes
Barbell Back Squat 3x6 40-50 kg total 2-2.5 min Full depth, heels flat, use the safety bars
Barbell Romanian Deadlift 3x8 40-50 kg total 2 min Hinge at the hips, flat back, slow lowering
Barbell Bench Press 3x8 30-40 kg total 2 min Stop each set with 2 reps left
Lat Pulldown or Assisted Pull-up 3x8-10 40-50 kg on the stack, or 30-40 kg of assistance 90 sec Pull the elbows down, shoulders away from the ears
Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press 3x8 10-14 kg each hand 90 sec Brace the core, don't arch the lower back
Dumbbell Box Step-up 3x8/leg Bodyweight, or 8-12 kg each hand 90 sec Knee-height box (about 45-50 cm), push through the heel of the working leg, lower for 2 seconds
Cable Pallof Press 3x10/side 5-10 kg 60 sec Anti-rotation core work, stand sideways to the cable
Copenhagen Plank (top leg on a box) 2x15-20 sec/side Bodyweight, knee on the box 60 sec Box at knee height, bottom leg hangs, hips in line with the shoulders. Move to the foot on the box once 20 sec is easy

Barbell weights are the total including the 20 kg bar. These are conservative estimates for a beginner of your size coming back after 2 months off. I don't know your actual strength, so treat them as a starting point and adjust with the steps below.

Finding Your Real Starting Weight

  • Week 1: do 2 sets per exercise at the low end of each range. After 2 months off, this keeps the soreness manageable and lets the technique come back first.
  • Week 2: go back to 3 sets. If the last rep of each set feels like you had 3 or more reps left, move up to the middle of the range.
  • Week 3 onward: pick the weight that leaves 2 reps in reserve on the last set, then follow the progression rules below.
  • If a set is a grind with form breaking down, drop the weight 10% rather than pushing through.

Progression

  • When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add weight next session: 2.5 kg total on the upper-body lifts, 5 kg total on squat and Romanian deadlift
  • Every 4th week, drop to 2 sets per exercise as a lighter week
  • If the rack is busy, swap squat and Romanian deadlift for goblet squat and dumbbell Romanian deadlift rather than skipping them

Recovery Day Workout

Profile: Beginner, 190cm / 100kg, full gym access
Time: about 35-40 minutes

A light session for the days when your muscles are still sore from a hard workout, usually the second day after. It keeps you moving without adding more load to tired legs.

When to Use It

  • You're 1-3 days after a hard session and your muscles feel tight and achy
  • The soreness is a dull ache that eases once you start moving
  • Not for sharp pain, joint pain, swelling or anything that gets worse as you move. Rest and see a doctor for those

If the soreness is only mild, do your normal session at the lighter week-1 settings instead (2 sets per exercise at the low end of each weight range).

Rules for This Session

  • Effort stays at about 4 out of 10. You should finish feeling better than you started
  • No jumps, no heavy legs, no training to failure
  • Stop an exercise if the movement hurts rather than aches
  • Skip the strength block entirely if your whole body is too sore, and just do the walk and the mobility

Part 1: Easy Walk (15-20 min)

Walk at a pace where you could hold a conversation. A treadmill or the street both work. Keep the incline flat or very low.

Part 2: Gentle Mobility (10 min)

The same primer as the main program, at half the usual range. Move slowly and stop before any deep stretch feels sharp.

  1. Ankle dorsiflexion rocks — 1x10 each side
  2. 90/90 hip switches — 1x8 each side, small range
  3. Couch stretch — 30 sec each side
  4. World's greatest stretch — 3 each side
  5. Lateral lunge to hamstring sweep — 5 each side, shallow
  6. Band lateral walks — 1x10 steps each direction, light band

Part 3: Light Upper Body and Core (10 min, optional)

Your arms and back are probably less sore than your legs, so this is a good place to keep some work going.

Exercise Sets x Reps Suggested weight Rest Notes
Seated Cable Row 2x15 20-30 kg on the stack 45 sec Light, squeeze the shoulder blades together
Machine Chest Press 2x15 20-30 kg on the stack 45 sec Smooth and slow, no grinding
Band Face Pull 2x15 Light band 45 sec Keeps the shoulders healthy
Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press 2x12 6-8 kg each hand 45 sec Stop well short of failure
Dead Bug 2x8/side Bodyweight 45 sec Lower back stays flat on the floor

After the Session

  • You should feel looser than before. If you feel worse, take a full rest day tomorrow
  • Drink water and eat a normal protein-rich meal
  • Go back to the regular program once the soreness is mild, starting with the lighter week-1 settings

Isometrics Recovery Session

Profile: Beginner, 190cm / 100kg, full gym access
Time: about 30-35 minutes

An alternative to the Recovery day session for the days when your muscles are still sore. Isometrics hold a position without lowering or lifting a weight. Most muscle soreness comes from the lowering part of exercises, so holds usually feel fine on sore muscles.

Rules for This Session

  • Effort stays at about 50-60% of your max. Don't squeeze as hard as you can
  • Hold in the middle of the range. Avoid positions that deeply stretch the sore muscle
  • Breathe normally the whole time and never hold your breath
  • If it hurts rather than aches, stop that exercise
  • Skip any exercise that makes the soreness worse for the rest of the day

Part 1: Easy Cardio (10 min)

Pick one machine and keep the effort easy enough to talk the whole time.

  • Simple bike: the best option for sore legs, low impact and almost no lowering phase
  • Treadmill: flat walk, no incline or downhill
  • Skip the echo bike and the rowing machine today, since both get hard quickly

Part 2: Gentle Mobility (5 min)

  1. Ankle dorsiflexion rocks — 1x10 each side
  2. 90/90 hip switches — 1x8 each side, small range
  3. World's greatest stretch — 3 each side

Part 3: Isometric Holds (15-20 min)

Legs and Ankles Isometrics

Profile: Beginner, 190cm / 100kg, full gym access
Time: about 25-30 minutes

An isometrics session built for sore legs and for ankle stability, which is one of your weak points. Isometrics hold a position without lowering or lifting a weight, so they are usually comfortable on sore muscles. Use it on the days after a hard leg session, or as a short ankle routine on any recovery day.

Rules for This Session

  • Effort stays at about 30-50% of your max for the ankle work and 50-60% for the legs. Never squeeze as hard as you can
  • Hold in the middle of the range, away from positions that stretch the sore muscle deeply
  • Breathe normally the whole time and never hold your breath
  • If it hurts rather than aches, stop that exercise
  • If an ankle is swollen, hurts to put weight on, or you recently twisted it, skip this session and have it checked

Part 1: Easy Cardio (5-10 min)

Use the simple bike or walk on a flat treadmill at a pace where you could talk the whole time. Skip the echo bike and the rowing machine today.

Do these seated or standing next to a wall. Push the foot against a wall, a bench leg, or a band anchored to a rack. Press gently and let nothing move.

  • Plan for 45-55 min on Lift A/C days and 40-50 min on Lift B as a practical round number.
  • Real-world variable not included: equipment/rack availability at the gym.

Nutrition Plan — Fat Loss (Training-Supportive)

Profile: 190cm, 100kg, 25-34, desk job, 3 lifts + 2 basketball sessions/week
Goal: Lose at least 5kg of fat while maintaining strength and performance

Your Numbers (Estimated)

Metric Estimate
BMR (resting energy needs) ~2,040 kcal/day
TDEE (maintenance, including training) ~2,950-3,050 kcal/day
Target intake (moderate deficit) ~2,550 kcal/day
Expected rate of loss ~0.4-0.5 kg/week
Time to lose 5kg ~10-12 weeks

These are starting estimates based on standard formulas (Mifflin-St Jeor), not a lab measurement — treat the number as a starting point to adjust from, not a fixed truth. Your actual TDEE could run 200-300 kcal higher or lower depending on things the formula can't see (NEAT, sleep, exact training intensity).

Why this deficit size: ~500 kcal/day below maintenance is aggressive enough to see real progress but conservative enough that you can still train hard, recover, and build/maintain muscle — which matters a lot given you're also trying to improve leg athleticism and strength. A bigger deficit would speed things up short-term but tends to tank training quality and makes muscle loss more likely.

Daily Targets

Macro Amount Calories Why
Protein ~200g ~800 kcal Preserves muscle in a deficit, keeps you full. ~2g/kg bodyweight
Fat ~75g ~675 kcal Minimum for hormone health, don't go much lower
Carbs ~270g ~1,075 kcal Fuels lifting + basketball performance — don't slash these to hit calories, cut fat/processed food instead
Total ~2,550 kcal

How to Actually Hit This

Protein (~200g/day) — the non-negotiable one:

  • Spread across 3-4 meals (~50g each) rather than back-loaded at dinner
  • Easy sources: chicken/turkey breast, Greek yogurt, eggs, whey protein, fish, lean beef, cottage cheese
  • If you're consistently under 150g, that's the first thing to fix before anything else

Carbs — time them around training:

  • Bigger carb portions on lift days and basketball days (pre and post-training)
  • Smaller portions on Sat/Sun rest days
  • Prioritize rice, oats, potatoes, fruit over refined sugar — same calories, but better for fullness and energy during training

Fat — fill the rest:

  • Olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish, whole eggs
  • Don't need to track this obsessively once protein and carbs are dialed in — it naturally lands where it lands

Weekly Adjustment Protocol

Body weight fluctuates day to day (water, sodium, digestion) — don't react to single-day numbers.

  1. Weigh yourself each morning, same conditions (post-bathroom, pre-food)
  2. Track the weekly average, not daily numbers
  3. Compare week to week:
    • Losing 0.3-0.6kg/week → on track, keep going
    • Losing faster than that → consider adding ~150-200 kcal back, especially if training quality is suffering
    • Not losing after 2-3 full weeks → cut ~150-200 kcal, usually from carbs/fat, not protein

Around Training Specifically

  • Lift/basketball days: eat a carb + protein meal 1-2 hours before if possible (e.g. rice + chicken, oats + protein shake)
  • Post-training: protein within a few hours matters more than immediately — don't stress the "anabolic window" myth, just get a solid protein meal in within a reasonable time
  • Don't train fasted on basketball days — explosive/skill work suffers more from low fuel than steady lifting does

Practical Notes

  • Alcohol: counts against your deficit and hurts recovery/sleep — not banned, but budget it in rather than ignoring it
  • Fiber/vegetables: not in the macro table above but eat plenty — helps fullness on fewer calories, doesn't cost you much room
  • Hydration: thirst gets mistaken for hunger often — aim for pale yellow urine as a rough gauge
  • Sleep: poor sleep makes fat loss noticeably harder (worse hunger signals, worse training recovery) — worth protecting as much as the diet itself

Reassessment Point

Re-run this whole calculation once you've lost ~5kg, since your maintenance calories will have dropped along with your weight. What works now won't be quite right at 95kg.
